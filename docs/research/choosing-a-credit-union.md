# Choosing a credit union: what readers weigh, what exists, how to show it

Research for the New Mexico credit union tracker. Compiled 2026-10-14. Every claim names its source. Where
we looked and found nothing solid, we say so. Figures marked "our check" come from NCUA's June 2026 call
report files in `.cache/` (FOICU, branch, ATM and FS220 tables), read for the 37 New Mexico credit unions.

## 1. What people weigh when they choose, join or switch

### Factors, ranked by how much evidence backs them

1. **Inertia: most people don't choose at all, they stay.** People keep a checking account an average of 19
   years and a savings account 17 (Bankrate/YouGov, 2,712 adults, Jan. 27-29, 2025, published 2025-03-10,
   https://www.bankrate.com/banking/checking-fees-survey/). Among non-members, the top barrier to joining a
   credit union is "satisfied with current bank" (58%), ahead of "don't know much about them" (26%), "no
   branches nearby" (22%), "unsure how to join" (14%) and "no real difference from banks" (12%) (Gallup Q1 2026
   U.S. panel, in a Callahan & Associates/Gallup webinar deck, 2026,
   https://go.callahan.com/rs/866-SES-086/images/Callahan-Gallup-Webinar-6-3-Presentation.pdf). The tool's
   job is partly to answer "is mine a good fit, or is it worth the hassle to move?"

2. **Location and convenience: the strongest and longest-running evidence.** In the Fed's Survey of Consumer
   Finances, branch location is the most-cited "most important reason" for choosing where to open a main
   checking account: 43% in 2019, and between 43% and 49% every survey since 1989 (Sakong and Zentefis,
   Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago policy brief, Nov. 2023,
   https://www.chicagofed.org/research/content-areas/mobility/policy-brief-bank-branch-location). Fed
   economists report the same: office location is the top reason in the SCF, over a third of responses
   across survey years (Anenberg et al., FEDS Notes, 2018-08-20,
   https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/why-are-there-still-bank-branches-20180820.html).
   Among people who stay put, 43% give a convenience reason (same account always, 18.3%; convenient
   branches/ATMs, 13.2%; too much hassle, 9.5%) (Bankrate/YouGov 2025, above). "Easy and convenient" ranks
   second (67) in Gallup's forced-choice importance scores (n=2,057; Gallup deck above).

3. **Trust and safety: ranked first when people are forced to trade off, but hard to measure.** "I trust
   them" scores 77, about 10 points ahead of every other attribute (Gallup deck above). Among fully engaged
   customers, "I feel my money is safe and secure with them" is the biggest single reason to choose or stay
   (40%) (same deck). "Don't trust banks" is the second-most-cited main reason households are unbanked
   (15.7%) (FDIC 2023 National Survey of Unbanked and Underbanked Households, about 30,000 households,
   released Nov. 2024, https://www.fdic.gov/household-survey).

4. **Fees: the clearest reason people leave or never open an account.** No/low monthly fees is tied for the
   top reason people stay (18.3%) (Bankrate/YouGov 2025). 31% of credit union members under 40 say they
   probably or definitely will leave in the next 12 months because of fees they were charged, vs. 25% of
   those 40 and older (J.D. Power 2025 U.S. Credit Union Satisfaction Study, 9,989 members, fielded Jan.
   2024-Jan. 2025, release 2025-04-01,
   https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250401833620/en/Trust-and-Convenience-Drive-Satisfaction-with-Credit-Unions-but-Overdraft-Fees-Present-Risks-J.D.-Power-Finds).
   In the 2026 study, 36% of members had a fee in the past quarter and only 39% said they understood their
   fee structure (down from 44%) (J.D. Power 2026 study, 10,386 members, released 2026-03-31, summarized at
   https://www.mddccua.org/2026/03/credit-unions-continue-to-outperform-banks-in-satisfaction-but-attrition-risk-is-rising-jd-power-finds/).
   One-third of unbanked households (33.4%) give a fee or minimum-balance reason as the main reason; "not
   enough money to meet minimum balance" is the top reason cited (42.3%) and top main reason (23.3%) (FDIC
   2023, report PDF at https://www.fdic.gov/household-survey).

5. **Rates: matter, but less than people assume for staying; more for switching and borrowing.**
   "Competitive rates / fees" scores 64 in Gallup's trade-off (combined item, so rates alone can't be
   separated). Only 4.3% of Bankrate respondents name good interest rates as why they stay. J.D. Power
   finds members "soft switch," opening accounts elsewhere and moving money: 59% have checking and 56% have
   savings at another institution (2026 study, above). We found no strong survey isolating loan rates as a
   reason to join; this is a gap.

6. **Service and people.** "Satisfied with customer service" is 16.3% of stay reasons (Bankrate/YouGov
   2025). J.D. Power scores credit unions on trust, people, banking flexibility, account offerings, saving
   time/money, digital channels and problem resolution; credit unions led banks on all of them in 2025 (729
   vs. 655 on a 1,000-point scale) and 2026 (725 vs. 657) (J.D. Power releases above).

7. **Digital banking: now how most households bank, but rarely the stated reason for choosing.** Almost half
   of banked households (48.3%) used mobile banking as their primary access in 2023, and 70.5% used an
   off-site channel (FDIC 2023). Only 5.7% name online/mobile tools as why they stay (Bankrate/YouGov 2025);
   "modern digital tools" scores 29 in Gallup's trade-off. Reads as a baseline expectation: its absence
   loses people (5% of non-members cite "poor technology," Gallup) more than its presence wins them.

8. **Eligibility and awareness: a barrier, not a preference.** 14% of non-members are unsure how to join and
   26% don't know much about credit unions (Gallup Q1 2026, above). A widely repeated figure that 40% of
   consumers think they can't join any credit union is attributed to "CUNA's Awareness Research" with no date
   or method (CUInsight/CUCollaborate, 2021, updated 2024,
   https://www.cuinsight.com/am-i-eligible-the-question-credit-unions-cant-afford-to-have-consumers-ask/);
   the same article's claim that 84% of online-application abandonment happens at the eligibility step has
   no source. Treat both as unverified.

9. **Values and community: high sentiment, low weight in choices.** America's Credit Unions reports 93% of
   consumers believe their credit union cares about the community (2026-02-20, no method published,
   https://www.americascreditunions.org/news-media/news/consumers-choose-credit-unions-affordability-security-opportunity).
   But "reflects values / community" is last (24) when people must trade off (Gallup deck). Only 17.8% of
   banked adults say banks and credit unions are truly different (Gallup, cited in the same Callahan
   materials, https://callahan.com/blog-good-enough-is-credit-unions-biggest-growth-challenge/).

### Lower-income, rural, Hispanic and Native American households

- **Hispanic households:** 9.5% unbanked in 2023 vs. 1.9% of White households; 14.8% of households but 33.4%
  of the unbanked. Yet 54.7% use mobile banking as their primary access, above White households (46.2%)
  (FDIC 2023 executive summary, https://www.fdic.gov/household-survey). Gaps held at every income level.
- **American Indian or Alaska Native households:** 12.2% unbanked (down from 23.0% in 2009); 48.9% mobile
  primary (FDIC 2023; FDIC warns the sample is small and estimates are noisy). The average straight-line
  distance from a reservation's center to the nearest bank branch was 12.2 miles and to an ATM 6.9 miles, in
  2013 data for the lower 48 (Native Nations Institute, "Access to Capital and Credit in Native Communities:
  A Data Review," 2016/2017,
  https://www.nnigovernance.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/2022-09/Accessing_Capital_and_Credit_in_Native_Communities__A_Data_Review.pdf).
  Barriers named for Native communities: ID requirements, distrust from history, distance (Pieratos,
  "Native Credit Unions: A Middle Path," Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis, 2019,
  https://www.minneapolisfed.org/~/media/files/community/indiancountry/native-credit-unions-middle-path-building-financial-access-capability-native-communities.pdf).
- **Rural and nonmetro:** 24.0% of nonmetro banked households use a teller as primary access vs. 13.8% in
  metro areas (FDIC 2023, Table 2.2). Rural communities are 10 times more likely than urban ones to be in a
  banking desert, and nearly 90% of rural households visited a branch in 2019 (CFPB data spotlight,
  2022-04-19,
  https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-releases-report-on-financial-challenges-facing-rural-communities/).
- **Lower-income:** people earning under $15,000 are roughly 25% more likely than those over $75,000 to use
  ATMs/tellers as their primary access (Chicago Fed brief, above). Minimum balances and unpredictable fees
  dominate the reasons for being unbanked (FDIC 2023).
- **Language, ID and hours (qualitative):** Latine interviewees (22 interviews, Chicago, 2023) valued staff
  from their community and in-person explanation, but couldn't get to branches open 9-5; only some
  institutions accept ITINs, and rules vary by branch (Godinez-Puig, Sonoda and Airi, Urban Institute, April
  2024,
  https://www.urban.org/sites/default/files/2024-04/How_Do_Financial_Attitudes_and_Preferences_Vary_among_Latine_Consumers.pdf).
  Small sample; not New Mexico. We found no New Mexico-specific survey of how residents choose a credit
  union.

## 2. Existing tools and the gaps

- **NCUA Credit Union Locator and Research a Credit Union** (https://mapping.ncua.gov/ResearchCreditUnion).
  Locator: search by name, address or charter number within a 5-60 mile radius, map pins, website, phone,
  drive-through/ATM; Research: financial information, insurance status, field-of-membership information,
  asset size, call report and FPR downloads (NCUA press release, 2015-09-01,
  https://ncua.gov/newsroom/press-release/2015/new-online-search-tool-makes-finding-credit-union-information-easier).
  The locator can filter on services such as bilingual services and financial counseling (CUInsight press
  item, https://www.cuinsight.com/press-release/ncua-enhances-credit-union-locator-and-research-a-credit-union-applications/).
  It's a lookup tool: one institution at a time, regulator vocabulary, no side-by-side and no plain-language
  "what this means for you."
- **MyCreditUnion.gov** (NCUA's consumer site) explains eligibility (employer, family, where you live, work,
  worship or attend school, groups) and points to the locator (https://mycreditunion.gov/brochure-publications/brochure/credit-union-right-me).
  General education, not comparison.
- **BlastPoint CU Scorecard** (https://cuscorecard.blastpoint.com/guide): free, NCUA call report data, 50+
  metrics, peer tiers, leaderboards and "performance signatures." Built for credit union executives and
  boards, not members (its guide says so; CU Today, 2026-02-04,
  https://www.cutoday.info/THE-feature/Free-Tool-Seeks-To-Change-How-Credit-Unions-Benchmark-Performance-Spot-Risk).
  Tiers start at $500 million, which leaves out most New Mexico credit unions.
- **Commercial safety grades:** DepositAccounts grades about 11,000 banks and credit unions A+ to F on
  capitalization, deposit growth and Texas ratio (https://www.depositaccounts.com/banks/health.aspx);
  BauerFinancial gives 0-5 stars free and "recommends" 4- and 5-star institutions
  (https://www.bauerfinancial.com/about-us/faqs/). These are exactly the grade-style judgments the Ledger is
  choosing not to make, and their formulas are proprietary.
- **NerdWallet / Bankrate "best" lists:** NerdWallet rates 100+ of the largest institutions on 60+ data points
  (APYs, fees, ATM/branch access, apps), with stated weights, e.g. savings: rate 50%, fees 25%
  (https://www.nerdwallet.com/l/ratings-methodology-for-banks-and-credit-unions). National and large-only;
  nearly all New Mexico credit unions are absent, and eligibility is a footnote.
- **New Mexico Financial Institutions Division** lists state-chartered credit unions
  (https://www.rld.nm.gov/financial-institutions/); a list, not a comparison.

**Gaps a local tool can fill:** all 37 New Mexico credit unions in one place, small ones included; "can I
join?" as the first filter; branches and ATMs near where the reader lives, including rural and tribal areas;
services that matter here (bilingual service, no-cost checking, payday-alternative loans, surcharge-free ATM
networks); context against similar New Mexico credit unions rather than national grades; and plain-language
explanations of what each number does and doesn't tell a member.

## 3. Design principles that apply

- **Overview first, zoom and filter, details on demand** (Shneiderman, "The Eyes Have It," IEEE Symposium on
  Visual Languages, 1996, https://www.cs.umd.edu/~ben/papers/Shneiderman1996eyes.pdf). Implication: open on
  a map or list of all 37, let readers filter to the ones they can join and reach, then open one.
- **Start from audience, goal and the one big idea; declutter; direct attention** (Knaflic, *Storytelling
  with Data*, Wiley, 2015; summary of the six lessons at
  https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/26535513-storytelling-with-data). Implication: each view answers one
  reader question ("Can I join?", "Is it near me?", "What does it cost?") with a sentence headline.
- **Tables for looking up and comparing individual values; graphs for shape and patterns** (Few, "Effectively
  Communicating Numbers," Perceptual Edge, 2005,
  https://www.perceptualedge.com/articles/Whitepapers/Communicating_Numbers.pdf). Implication: the
  side-by-side comparison is a table; trends and "where it sits among peers" are small charts.
- **Match the most important attribute to the most accurate channel; position on a common scale is best**
  (Munzner, *Visualization Analysis and Design*, CRC Press, 2014; slides at
  https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~tmm/talks/vad/VAD-marks.pdf). Implication: strip or dot plots on a shared axis for
  "where one sits," not color-coded maps or gauges.
- **Small multiples and high data density** (Tufte, *Envisioning Information*, Graphics Press, 1990, and *The
  Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, 1983, https://www.edwardtufte.com/tufte/books_vdqi).
  Implication: the same small chart repeated for each selected credit union, same scales.
- **Choice overload depends on difficulty and preference uncertainty.** A meta-analysis of 99 observations
  (N = 7,202) finds overload is driven by choice-set complexity, task difficulty, preference uncertainty and
  an effort-minimizing goal (Chernev, Böckenholt and Goodman, *Journal of Consumer Psychology*, 2015,
  https://myscp.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1016/j.jcps.2014.08.002). Implication: 37 options is
  manageable if eligibility and distance cut it to a handful first.
- **Screen, then compare.** Interactive decision aids that screen options and then put a few in a
  standardized comparison matrix improved decision quality and cut effort (Häubl and Trifts, *Marketing
  Science* 19(1), 2000, https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mksc.19.1.4.15178). Implication:
  cap the comparison at three or four.
- **Less is more, especially for less numerate readers.** Showing fewer, more meaningful measures improved
  comprehension and choices in three experiments (Peters, Dieckmann, Dixon, Hibbard and Mertz, *Medical Care
  Research and Review* 64(2), 2007, https://doi.org/10.1177/10775587070640020301).
- **Numbers need evaluative meaning to be used.** Adding reference lines and evaluative labels made people,
  especially the less numerate, actually use numeric information (Peters et al., "Bringing Meaning to
  Numbers," *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied* 15(3), 2009,
  https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/xap153213.pdf). Tension with our no-grades rule: give meaning
  through neutral references (New Mexico median, peer middle half) and legal thresholds, not good/bad labels.
- **Inform, don't persuade; disclose uncertainty; state evidence quality** (Blastland, Freeman, van der
  Linden, Marteau and Spiegelhalter, "Five rules for evidence communication," *Nature*, 2020-11-18,
  https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-020-03189-1).
- **Presenting financial risk without causing a run.** Fully insured retail depositors generally did not
  run in the 2023 failures, and at times had net inflows; large and uninsured depositors ran (Chu et al.,
  "Dissecting Depositor Flight," FDIC staff study, May 2026,
  https://www.fdic.gov/center-financial-research/staff-studies-may-2026.pdf). In an earlier bank run,
  uninsured depositors, borrowers and staff ran first (Iyer, Puri and Ryan, "A Tale of Two Runs," *Journal of
  Finance*, 2016, working paper at
  https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/conference/2013/stable_funding/depositors_Iyer_et_al.pdf).
  NCUA: every member has at least $250,000 in coverage, backed by the full faith and credit of the United
  States, and "members have never lost even a penny of insured savings at a federally insured credit
  union" (https://ncua.gov/support-services/share-insurance-fund). After 2023, the FDIC ran an awareness
  campaign because many consumers didn't understand coverage (FDIC release,
  https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USFDIC/bulletins/3753a04). Implication: put insurance status and
  what it means next to every safety measure; describe capital against NCUA's own legal line ("well
  capitalized" is a net worth ratio of 7% or more, 12 CFR part 702,
  https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-VII/subchapter-A/part-702; larger "complex" credit unions
  face added risk-based tests); no red alarms or "at risk" lists.

## 4. Can public data answer each factor?

| Factor | Answerable from public data? | Source and caveats |
|---|---|---|
| Eligibility / field of membership | **Partly.** | FOICU `TOM_CODE` gives a membership type (community, rural district, multiple common bond, etc.; code list at https://www.cucollaborate.com/blog/what-the-heck-is-a-tom-code). Our check: 18 of 37 New Mexico credit unions carry code 99, "state charter," which says nothing about who can join. Account 084 gives potential members. The written field of membership (which counties, employers, groups) is not in the files; it has to come from each credit union's site or bylaws. Research a Credit Union shows some FOM information; we did not confirm how much. |
| Branch and ATM locations | **Yes.** | "Credit Union Branch Information" (address, hours, drive-through, ATM, shared branching) and "ATM Locations" files. Our check: 189 site records for New Mexico credit unions (178 in New Mexico) and 143 ATM records. Surcharge-free ATM network yes/no and name (PP0034/PP0035): 22 of 37 say yes. Self-reported; not verified. |
| Loan rates | **Partly.** | Call report reports the loan rate "being offered" as of quarter end, the "most common rate" per category (new auto, used auto, credit card, unsecured, first mortgage, etc.) (5300 instructions, Sept. 2024, https://ncua.gov/files/publications/regulations/call-report-instructions-september-2024.pdf). Our check: new and used auto rates filled for all 37. One number per category, not by credit score or term; not an APR quote. |
| Savings / dividend rates | **No (since 2022).** | NCUA retired the money market and share certificate dividend rate accounts in March 2022, saying rates "can be obtained from the credit unions" (NCUA catalog of account code changes, March 2022, https://ncua.gov/files/publications/regulations/call-report-changes-march-2022.pdf). Our check: regular share and certificate rate fields are empty for all 37. Dividends paid divided by shares gives an average cost of funds, which is not a rate a reader can get. |
| Fees | **Only in aggregate.** | Total fee income (account 131) per credit union. NCUA stopped publishing individual credit unions' overdraft and NSF fee income from the March 2025 cycle; it now collects it in exams and publishes only totals (Consumer Finance Monitor, 2025-03-05, https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2025/03/05/ncua-rescinds-policy-to-publish-credit-unions-overdraft-and-nsf-income/; NCUA, https://ncua.gov/newsroom/press-release/2025/hauptman-announces-changes-ncuas-overdraftnsf-fee-collection). Our check: overdraft fee income empty for all 37. Fee schedules (monthly, overdraft, ATM) exist only on credit union sites and disclosures. Profile flags exist for "no cost share drafts" (28 of 37 yes) and "overdraft protection/courtesy pay." |
| Digital services | **Yes, as yes/no.** | Profile items: online banking (33), mobile app (30), remote deposit capture (28), person-to-person (28), bill pay, online member application (24), e-statements. Self-reported; says nothing about quality. App store ratings are public but not in NCUA data. |
| Language and inclusion services | **Partly.** | "Bilingual services" flag (24 of 37), financial counseling, credit builder, payday alternative loans, international remittances; low-income designation (`LIMITED_INC`, 31 of 37) and minority depository institution flag (`IsMDI`, 12 of 37). Which languages, and ITIN acceptance, are not reported. |
| Complaints | **No, for New Mexico.** | CFPB's database does not publish complaints referred to other regulators, "such as complaints about depository institutions with less than $10 billion in assets" (https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/consumer-complaints/). The largest New Mexico credit union reported about $5.0 billion in June 2026 (our check), so none appear. NCUA's complaint dataset is listed as non-public (https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/consumer-assistance-and-complaint-data). A records request for counts is the only route we found. |
| Safety | **Yes, with care.** | Insured status, net worth ratio and NCUA's capital category (account 700), delinquency, charge-offs, earnings: all in the call report. CAMELS ratings and exam findings are confidential and must stay out. |
| Service quality, trust | **No.** | J.D. Power covers only the 29 largest credit unions nationally; no New Mexico credit union-level satisfaction data found. |

## Implications for the tracker

1. **Lead with "Can I join?"** Ask county, employer or group first and filter to eligible credit unions.
   Because 18 of 37 have no useful membership code, the written fields of membership must be collected by
   hand from each credit union and dated.
2. **Then "Is it near me?"** A map of branches and ATMs with a distance filter, and a note on shared
   branching and surcharge-free networks; this matters most for rural, tribal and teller-reliant readers.
3. **Overview, then a short list, then side-by-side.** Open on all 37; let filters cut it to a handful;
   compare at most three or four in a table with the same rows for each.
4. **Show few measures, each tied to a reader question:** cost (no-cost checking, fee income per member),
   borrowing (most common auto and mortgage rate), access (branches, ATMs, digital services), language and
   inclusion services, safety. Keep the full call report behind "details."
5. **Give numbers meaning without grades:** place each credit union on a shared strip next to the New Mexico
   median and its peer group's middle half, and say in words where it sits ("above most similar New Mexico
   credit unions"). No stars, letters, colors for good/bad or overall rank.
6. **Use legal lines where they exist.** Capital is shown against NCUA's 7% "well capitalized" line, with
   the category NCUA assigns, not our own threshold.
7. **Put deposit insurance first in the safety section,** in plain words ($250,000 per member per ownership
   category, federal backing, no insured member has lost money), before any capital or delinquency figure.
   Avoid red alerts and watch lists; explain changes over time calmly with the date of the data.
8. **Label loan rates honestly:** "the most common rate the credit union reported offering on June 30," not
   "your rate"; link to the credit union's current rates. Say plainly that NCUA no longer collects savings
   rates and that individual overdraft fee figures are no longer public.
9. **Add services that matter in New Mexico as filters:** bilingual service, no-cost checking, payday
   alternative loans, credit builder loans, remittances, low-income and minority designations. Note that
   flags are self-reported.
10. **Build in Spanish and for phones.** Hispanic and Native households use mobile banking as their main
    channel at least as often as White households (FDIC 2023), and bilingual service is one of the filters.
11. **Address inertia directly:** a "Check your credit union" path that shows a reader's current credit union
    against the ones they could join nearby, since most people never shop around.
12. **State what the tool can't tell you** on every page: fees, savings rates, service quality and complaints
    aren't in public data; tell readers which questions to ask a credit union before joining.
